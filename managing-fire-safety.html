INTRODUCTION

Effective fire safety management is not achieved through isolated assessments or reactive works.

This page outlines the core principles that underpin sustainable fire safety management within housing organisations.

IT REQUIRES

Clear allocation of responsibility

Competent decision-making

Structured risk prioritisation

Documented governance

Proportionate control measures

How to Manage Fire Safety Systematically

Fire safety should operate as a structured management system, not a series of disconnected actions.

  • Maintaining an accurate building register
  • Operating a risk-based FRA programme
  • Converting findings into prioritised workstreams
  • Monitoring delivery against risk
  • Reviewing effectiveness periodically

The objective is clarity and control of life safety risk — not simply compliance activity.

How to Ensure Competence

Competence applies to:

APPLIES TO

  • Fire risk assessors
  • Reviewing officers
  • Contractors
  • Internal decision-makers

Organisations should be able to demonstrate:

  • How assessors are selected
  • How quality is reviewed
  • How internal staff are trained
  • How technical decisions are escalated
Competence is not assumed — it must be evidenced.

How to Maintain the Golden Thread

Effective fire safety management depends on reliable information.

This includes:

  • Accurate building data
  • Up-to-date FRA records
  • Action trackers
  • Evidence of completed works
  • Fire strategy documentation
  • Inspection and testing records

Information should be:

  • Accessible
  • Structured
  • Traceable
  • Capable of being audited

The goal is defensible record keeping, not excessive documentation.

How to Approach Fire Prevention

Fire prevention is often overlooked in favour of post-assessment action management.

Prevention measures should include:

  • Electrical safety management
  • Control of ignition sources
  • Resident engagement
  • Contractor control
  • Monitoring of repeat incident patterns
Reducing fire incidence is as important as managing consequences.

How to Comply with Fire Safety Legislation

Compliance requires more than completing fire risk assessments. Responsible Persons must be able to demonstrate that they:

  • Identify risks
  • Act on findings
  • Maintain fire precautions
  • Appoint competent persons
  • Keep records where required
  • Review arrangements periodically
Fire safety management should be aligned with guidance published by government under Article 50 of the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order, and interpreted proportionately to the building type and risk profile. Compliance should be structured, documented and capable of explanation.

How to Determine and Implement Evacuation Strategies

Evacuation strategy selection must be:

  • Building-specific
  • Risk-informed
  • Technically justified
  • Communicated clearly

Common strategies include:

  • Stay put
  • Simultaneous evacuation
  • Phased evacuation
  • Progressive horizontal evacuation
    (within specialised housing settings)

The chosen strategy must align with:

  • Compartmentation standards
  • Detection and alarm arrangements
  • Resident profile
  • Management capability

It should be periodically reviewed and embedded into operational procedures.
